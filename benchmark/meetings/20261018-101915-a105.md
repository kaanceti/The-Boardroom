## Agenda

I've built a Notion student planner. Should I launch it on Etsy now in October or wait until January?

## Statements

### Skeptic (Risk and failure analysis)

**Statement from Skeptic**

My view: the real danger is not the launch date. It is launching a product that looks like thousands of others.

**Risks, ranked by likelihood and impact**

1. **Nobody finds it.** A new shop has zero reviews and zero sales history. Etsy buries those. Most new digital shops make no sales in the first 30 to 60 days. I can't give you an exact rate. Treat this as the most likely cause of failure.
2. **Crowded niche.** Notion student planners are one of the most copied digital products on Etsy. Many sell for $5 to $15. If yours is a generic dashboard with a to-do list and a GPA tracker, price pressure will push you toward $5 or less.
3. **Thin margins on a low price.** Etsy takes a $0.20 listing fee, about 6.5% transaction fee, and payment processing of roughly 3% plus $0.25 per sale. I'm going from memory. Check current rates. On a $9 sale, you keep about $7.90 before ads and taxes.
4. **Support and breakage.** Buyers often get confused by the "duplicate" link. Notion can change features. Each confused buyer costs you time, and one bad review early hurts a lot.
5. **Rights and policy.** Check licenses on fonts, icons, and cover images. Check Etsy's rules on digital goods and on disclosing how items are made. Check whether Notion's brand rules limit your listing images or name. I'm unsure of the current wording, so read them before you list.
6. **Seasonality.** Back-to-school peak was roughly July to September. A second bump usually comes in late December and January, when new semesters start. I have no data in hand, so verify it with Etsy search trends or Google Trends.

**October versus January**

Waiting has a concrete cost. You lose about 10 weeks of ranking history and reviews. In January, you would launch into the second peak with no track record while every competitor refreshes their listings.

Launching now costs very little. One listing is $0.20, plus your time. The downside is a weak first impression if the product is not ready. That is the only reason to wait.

**What would have to be true for this to work**

- Your planner does something specific that most don't. Examples: a nursing student version, a university-specific one, or a semester-by-semester GPA planner.
- It works on Notion's free plan.
- You have at least 5 clear listing images and a short video or GIF.
- Your price is $12 or higher, or you have a bundle that justifies it.
- You can get 5 or more honest reviews by mid-December. Friends, a student group, or a discount to early buyers can help, as long as you follow Etsy's review rules.
- You can answer buyer messages within 24 hours.

**What I'm unsure of**

I don't know your niche, your price, your traffic source, or whether you have other listings. Those matter more than the month.

Bottom line: Launch now as a soft launch, because waiting costs ranking history and launching costs about $0.20. Treat October to December as a test, and refresh the listing in early December for the January peak. If the product is not clearly different from the top 20 results, fix that first, not the date.

### Advocate (Opportunity and upside)

**Advocate's statement: launch in October**

My case is simple. Launch now. Waiting gives up the one thing you can't buy later, which is time.

**Why now beats January**

- Etsy listings start with no reviews and no sales history. It usually takes weeks to learn what buyers click on. If you list on Oct 25, you get about 10 weeks of learning before the January rush.
- If you wait, you launch into the busiest week with zero reviews, against sellers who already have hundreds.
- Your product is digital. It has no inventory and no shipping. A weak launch costs you little, so the downside of going early is small.
- October gives you real use cases. Midterms and finals planning run through November and December. You can market it as a "finals survival planner" and a "next semester setup" in the same listing.
- Black Friday is Nov 26 to 30 this year. A listing with a few reviews can run a small sale then. A brand new listing can't.

**The best version of this**

- Start with one clear niche, not "student planner." For example, nursing students, college freshmen, or high schoolers with ADHD. Narrow listings rank easier and convert better.
- Add a free or low-price version (about $5) as a way in. Sell the full bundle (about $15 to $25) as the main product.
- Make 5 to 8 mockup images and a short video. On Etsy, images do most of the selling.
- If it sells, build a family of products: a semester add-on, a budget tracker, a grad school pack. Each new listing brings in new traffic and points back to the others.

**Your advantages**

- It's already built. Most people stall before this step.
- You can fix problems fast, since you control the whole product.
- Buyer questions and reviews will tell you what to build next.

**Where the upside is limited**

- Notion planners are a crowded category on Etsy. I haven't checked current listing counts or prices, so treat my price ranges as rough guesses.
- Realistic first-season income is probably tens to a few hundred dollars, not thousands. I'm unsure, and it depends heavily on your niche and images.
- Etsy takes fees. I believe it's $0.20 per listing, 6.5% per sale, plus payment processing. Please verify the current numbers.
- The product depends on Notion, which can change its template features.

**What I'm unsure about**

I haven't pulled search volume or competitor data. Scout should check whether "Notion student planner" demand really peaks in January or in August and September.

**A simple test**

List by Oct 31. If you have zero sales and low views after 4 weeks, rework the title and images before January. That is still a better outcome than finding out in January.

Bottom line: Launch in October, in a narrow niche, by Oct 31, because early reviews and learning time are worth more than waiting for the January peak.

### Scout (Evidence and fact-checking)

**Evidence on launching a Notion student planner on Etsy: October or January**

**What the evidence says**

- **Fees.** Third-party guides give these US fees: $0.20 per listing, renewed every 4 months. The transaction fee is 6.5%. Payment processing is 3% plus $0.25 per order. Offsite Ads take 12% to 15% of a sale only if the sale comes through them. These guides are not Etsy's own fee page, so check etsy.com before you price. Sources: [Checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees), [Craftybase](https://app.craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees).

- **Back-to-school timing.** Seller guides put the peak from mid-July to late August, with a smaller wave in September. They advise listing 6 to 8 weeks before the peak, which is June. Sources: [Better Listing](https://getbetterlisting.com/en/blog/2026-etsy-back-to-school-seo), [Promptless Press](https://www.promptlesspress.com/blog-back-to-school-printables-sell-etsy).
- **Demand after the peak.** One guide says demand holds at a lower level through the school year. That is the case for October. It is a single seller blog, not hard data.
- **Calendar-year demand.** I found no sourced data on a January spike for planners or Notion templates. New Year planner demand is widely believed, but I could not confirm it. Notion templates may behave differently from printable planners.

**Common claims that are weak or outdated**

- "You must launch before the peak or the product fails." That is false. Digital files can be edited and relisted at any time. The same source says so.
- "Etsy fees are 20 to 25% of every sale." This is misleading. That figure assumes Offsite Ads fire. Without ads, the fees are about 10 to 12% plus $0.25.
- "January is the best time for planners." I could not verify this. The sources I found were about back-to-school and holidays only.

**What I am unsure of**

- I found no Notion-specific seasonality data. All timing evidence is for printables and general digital goods.
- The sources are seller blogs. They may be selling something, and they disagree with each other on dates (June 1, mid-July, or August).
- New listings need time to build reviews and search ranking. I have no number for how long that takes.

**A way to settle it with data**

1. Search "Notion student planner" on Etsy and note the top 10 listings. Record their sales counts and review dates.
2. Check Google Trends for "notion planner" over 5 years. It is free.
3. Publish in October anyway. The cost is about $0.20 per listing. Then relaunch with new photos in early January and compare.

Bottom line: Launch in October, because waiting saves about $0.20 per listing and gains nothing proven. I found no Notion-specific data showing January is better, so use October and January as a free test.
