# Blind test: every question and answer

Fifteen questions, each answered by a single Claude call and by a full Boardroom meeting, then judged blind by Claude Opus. Method and summary: [benchmark/README.md](README.md) and the main [README](../README.md#how-it-got-here).

- **Round 1** judged the board's original resolution. **Round 2** re-ran only the Chair on the same meetings with the reworked write-up; the members' statements and the single answers are identical in both rounds.
- Scores are out of 10, averaged over two judgements with the order swapped. A win only counts when both orders agree.
- Member names were replaced with neutral labels ("Analyst 1" and so on) before judging; the answers below are shown as the board wrote them. Full meeting minutes are in [meetings/](meetings/).
- Four questions were about my own life, so their details and answers are withheld for privacy. Their scores still count in every total.

<summary>Board answer, round 2 (reworked Chair)</summary>

#### Decision
Launch in October, by Oct 31, as a soft launch in one narrow niche. Do not wait for January. Treat October to December as a cheap test, and set a stop-or-continue date of Jan 31.

#### Why
- Launching costs about $0.20 per listing plus your time. Waiting saves almost nothing and gains no proven benefit.
- You get about 10 weeks of views, favorites, and search data before January. Reviews help conversion, so early ones are worth having.
- The board found no Notion-specific data showing January beats other months. Seller blogs put the back-to-school peak between June and August, and they disagree. A January bump is plausible but unconfirmed.
- Fees are modest without ads: about 6.5% plus 3% plus $0.25 per sale, plus $0.20 per listing. A $12 sale nets about $10.40. Confirm on etsy.com/legal/fees.

#### Main risks
- **No differentiation.** Notion student planners are a crowded category. A generic planner will not sell at any date.
- **Zero traffic.** New shops often get few views at first. I have no reliable rate.
- **Review rules.** Etsy bans paying or rewarding buyers for reviews. Friend and family reviews risk a flag. Do not offer discounts in exchange for reviews.
- **Brand and content rights.** Check Notion's brand rules for your listing name and images. Check licenses on fonts, icons, and covers. Check Etsy's rules on disclosing AI use.
- **Money.** New sellers may face payout holds and a setup fee of about $15. If your annual sales pass $10,000, Offsite Ads become mandatory and take 12% to 15% of attributed sales.
- **Link sharing.** Anyone with the duplicate link can pass it on. Notion can also change template features.
- **Free competition.** Many free Notion templates exist, and that caps your price.

#### Honest criticism for the owner
You did not tell us what makes your planner different. That matters more than the month. You also did not give your weekly hours, your budget, or your price. Expect $0 to $100 by mid-January. A realistic year-one range is a few hundred dollars, perhaps up to $2,000 with several listings. These are estimates, not data. Finishing the build was the easy part. Winning attention is the hard part.

#### Next steps
1. By Oct 22: do a 30-minute check. Search Etsy for your niche and note the top 10 listings' prices and review counts. Check Google Trends for "notion planner" over 5 years. If you cannot name one clear difference, fix that first.
2. By Oct 31: open the shop and publish with 5 or more images and a short video. Price at $10 to $15. Test the duplicate link on a second account and confirm your free-plan compatibility. Start identity and bank setup now, since it can take days.
3. Check stats on Nov 28 (under 100 views means rewrite the title and main image). Refresh by Dec 15 by editing the same listing, not relisting. Decide on Jan 31 whether to continue or stop.

#### Board notes
- I overruled the claim that new listings are buried by ranking. Etsy does not say this. The case for October rests on cost and learning time.
- I overruled the "January relaunch test." It changes season and photos together, so it proves little.
- Open: whether a lower launch price ($8 to $10) beats $12 or more for early sales. No one checked live prices.

<summary>Board answer, round 2 (reworked Chair)</summary>

#### Decision
Skip the Google Data Analytics certificate. Learn Python, but only after a 1-week check of your own SQL level and your team's tools. Use a real work task as the project, and aim to finish it by 11 January 2027.

#### Why
- You already have the job. The certificate is built for people seeking a first role, and it covers spreadsheets, SQL, Tableau and R. Most detailed reviews say it does not teach Python. I could not confirm this on Google's own page.
- The certificate costs about 240 to 260 hours (6 months at 10 hours a week) and roughly $300 at about $49 a month. These are recalled figures, so check Coursera.
- Python is free to learn. A real work task makes it stick far better than course exercises.
- Job posting surveys put SQL ahead of Python (SQL about 53% to 90%, Python about 31% to 55%). The surveys are rough and disagree. So if your SQL is weak, fix that first.

#### Main risks
- **Learning without a use.** If you cannot name one task to automate within 30 days, you will likely forget Python.
- **Company rules.** Check that you may run scripts, export data and install software. Do not move sensitive or personal data to personal machines or public GitHub. That is a legal and job risk.
- **Silent errors.** An automated report with a bug can reach management. Run it beside the manual version for 2 to 3 cycles.
- **Time slippage.** Work crunch can cost 2 to 3 weeks. Plan for that.
- **Money.** Low. Kaggle and pandas docs are free. Coursera is the only real spend, and you can avoid it.

#### Honest criticism for the owner
You asked "which course," but you did not say what you want. Promotion, a new job and a move toward data science need different skills. You also gave no SQL level, no team tools and no weekly hours. Without those, no timeline can be trusted. Choosing a credential is also an easy way to feel productive without changing your work. A finished project at your job counts for more.

#### Next steps
1. This week, spend 1 hour: rate your SQL, ask your manager what tools the team uses and whether Python is allowed, and read 5 postings for the role you want next. If SQL is weak or Python is not wanted, do SQL first.
2. Block 3 sessions of 2 hours a week from Monday 19 October 2026. Use Kaggle Learn's free Python and Pandas courses, then your own work data (with permission).
3. Pick one recurring report to automate. Checkpoint on 11 January 2027: one finished, checked project. If not done, find out why before spending any money.

<summary>Board answer, round 2 (reworked Chair)</summary>

#### Decision
Do not move for the £4,000. Move only if the startup shows at least 18 months of cash runway in evidence you can check yourself, and the role gives you scope or a title you cannot get where you are within two years. If either test fails, stay.

#### Why
- The raise is small. £4,000 gross is about £2,320 a year after tax and National Insurance at the 40% band, and about £2,880 at the 20% band. That is roughly £190 to £240 a month. Student loan repayments would cut it further.
- Lost benefits can erase it. A 5% employer pension match on a £40,000 salary is £2,000 a year. Bonus, health cover, sick pay and holiday days also count. Salary is unknown, so that figure is only an example.
- The downside is much larger than the upside. A 3-month gap on £2,500 a month take-home costs £7,500, which is about three years of the raise.
- Real upside exists, but it is in learning and scope, not pay. That is only worth taking if the role is clearly bigger than yours.

#### Main risks
- **Job loss:** The startup may cut staff or run out of money. No reliable survival figure exists for funded startups. The 38% five-year survival figure covers all new businesses, including sole traders, so it overstates the risk.
- **Legal:** Unfair dismissal protection currently needs 2 years of service. The Employment Rights Act 2025 is reported to cut this to 6 months for dismissals from 1 January 2027. I have not verified this myself. If you move, your clock restarts. You would likely have no protection for the first months, and probation terms may allow quick dismissal.
- **Redundancy pay:** Statutory redundancy pay needs 2 years' service. Whether that is changing is unconfirmed. Check gov.uk.
- **Equity:** Treat it as worth zero until written terms prove otherwise.
- **Savings:** Moving with less than 3 to 6 months of expenses saved is unsafe.

#### Honest criticism for the owner
You are focused on the number, and the number is the weakest part of the offer. You also have not said what your current job offers for growth, or what the new role actually involves. Without those, you cannot judge this. Do not assume you can return to your old employer. Asking for a counter-offer can also mark you as a leaver, so do it only if you would genuinely accept it.

#### Next steps
1. **By 25 October:** Ask the startup for last funding date and amount, months of cash, and why the role is open. Check Companies House filings yourself. Do not rely on the founder's word. Also ask how long the offer stays open.
2. **By 1 November:** Get the written offer. Compare pension, bonus, notice period, probation terms, redundancy terms and any equity (share count, strike price, vesting, whether it is EMI) line by line with your current package.
3. **By 15 November:** Confirm you have 3 to 6 months of expenses saved, then decide. Walk away if runway is under 18 months or the total package is not clearly better.

- One member's point that employer pension contributions can be lost was overruled. UK employer contributions normally belong to you at once.
