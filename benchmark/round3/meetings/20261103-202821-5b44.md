## Statements

### Skeptic (Risk and failure analysis)

The usual answer: take the shop if your market sales are strong, because a fixed base builds a brand.

You didn't say which answer you hope for, but asking about a specific unit suggests you lean yes. So I have judged this as if you hoped for no.

**What £1,200 a month really costs**

- Rent alone is £14,400 a year.
- Add service charge, buildings insurance recharge, utilities, card fees, product liability and contents insurance, and possibly business rates. A realistic total is £18,000 to £24,000 a year. I can't be precise without the lease and the unit's rateable value.
- Small Business Rate Relief may cut rates to zero if the rateable value is under £12,000. Check this on the VOA website before anything else.
- Landlords often want a 3 to 6 month deposit (£3,600 to £7,200) and a personal guarantee. Fit-out could cost another £2,000 to £5,000.

**Break-even, on assumed figures**

- Say a candle sells for £20 and £12 of that is gross profit. You would need about 1,500 to 2,000 extra candles a year just to stand still.
- That is 30 to 40 more a week, every week, on top of what markets already give you.
- I don't know your current sales. If you don't sell close to that at markets now, the shop starts in deficit.

**Risks, ranked by likelihood and impact**

1. **Seasonality (high, high).** Candles peak October to December. Signing now catches one Christmas, then you pay £1,200 a month through a slow January to August.
2. **Lost market income (high, high).** A shop open Tuesday to Saturday takes your weekends. Unless you hire someone at about £12.21 an hour (National Living Wage from April 2025), you swap proven market takings for unproven footfall.
3. **Lease lock-in (medium, very high).** Commercial leases often run 3 to 5 years with a personal guarantee. Failure then means personal debt, not just a closed shop. This is the most likely way it ends badly.
4. **Footfall (medium, high).** Market customers came for a market. A side-street unit may get few walk-ins.
5. **Compliance and fire (low to medium, high).** Lit testers and stock storage may breach lease or insurance terms. GB CLP labelling for fragrance allergens applies wherever you sell, but a shop draws more scrutiny from Trading Standards.
6. **Burnout (medium, medium).** Making, selling and running a shop alone is a lot of hours.

**Cautious options, best first**

- Do nothing yet. Keep the markets and track monthly profit through to March 2027.
- Take a short pop-up licence for November and December only. Many councils and landlords offer these.
- Rent a shelf in a shared maker shop for £50 to £150 a month to test demand away from markets.

**What would have to be true for the shop to work**

- Your current net profit already covers about £20,000 a year of extra overheads with room to spare.
- The lease has a break clause at 12 months or less, and no personal guarantee.
- The unit's rateable value qualifies for full relief.
- Someone other than you can staff it, or markets can stop without a fall in income.

If you can't confirm all four, the case for caution is strong, not marginal.

Bottom line: Don't sign a standard lease now. Test with a two-month pop-up or a shared shelf first, and only commit if a break clause and your numbers both hold up.

## Chair's resolution

## Decision
No, do not sign for this unit on a standard lease now. First test whether people buy your candles away from markets. Use a shared maker shelf (about £50 to £150 a month) or a short licence from January to March 2027. Only take a unit if the test passes, the lease has a break clause at 12 months or sooner, and there is no personal guarantee.

## Why
- **The shop has to sell a lot.** £1,200 rent plus utilities, insurance, card fees and maybe business rates comes to about £1,450 to £1,700 a month. At 60 to 65% gross margin, you would need about £2,500 to £2,850 in sales a month before you pay yourself. That is roughly 180 candles a month, or 7 a day.
- **It is a hidden swap, not extra income.** You work alone. Shop hours will clash with your weekend markets, so the shop must earn its costs plus the market profit you give up. Paying staff instead costs about £2,200 a month for 40 hours a week at £12.71 an hour, which is more than the rent. Check the current wage rate on gov.uk.
- **Market demand may not move with you.** Market shoppers came for the market. Nobody knows yet if they will find a side-street unit.
- **Setup costs.** Expect £6,000 to £8,000 up front for the deposit, fit-out, stock and legal fees. These are estimates.

## Main risks
- **Personal guarantee.** If you sign one on a multi-year lease and the shop fails, the debt becomes yours personally, not just the business's. This is the most likely way this ends badly.
- **Seasonality.** Candles peak from October to December. If you sign a lease after a strong Christmas, you then carry £1,200 a month through January to September.
- **Exit costs.** Many leases make you pay to restore the unit when you leave (called dilapidations). Some also remove your right to renew the lease under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954.
- **Fire and insurance.** Pouring wax, lit testers and workshops may break the lease's use clause or the landlord's insurance terms. You need product and public liability cover.
- **Business rates are unclear.** In England, units with a rateable value under £12,000 currently pay nothing under Small Business Rate Relief. Rules changed in April 2026 and the board could not confirm the details. Scotland and Wales have different rules.

## Honest criticism for the owner
You asked about a specific unit without knowing your own numbers. No one on the board could judge this, because you have not said what your markets earn after pitch fees. A shop decision without that figure is a guess. Also, do not treat Christmas sales as proof. December will flatter any test.
