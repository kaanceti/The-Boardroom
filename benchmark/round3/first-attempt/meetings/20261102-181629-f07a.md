## Agenda

I sell handmade candles at weekend markets. Should I rent a small shop unit for £1,200 a month?

## Statements

### Skeptic (Risk and failure analysis)

**The usual answer:** "Run the numbers, and if markets are going well, take the shop to grow." I argue for not signing a lease yet, and possibly never.

**The core problem**
£1,200 a month is the visible cost, not the real one. A typical UK small unit also brings:
- VAT on rent if the landlord has opted to tax: £1,440 a month.
- Business rates. These could be zero under Small Business Rate Relief if the rateable value is under £12,000. Check the VOA listing before anything else.
- Service charge, utilities, buildings and contents insurance, and card fees: roughly £250 to £500 a month. This is my estimate.
- A deposit of 3 to 6 months' rent, plus legal fees of about £1,000 to £2,000.
- Fit-out and shelving: £1,000 to £5,000.

Realistic total: about £1,600 to £2,000 a month, or £19,000 to £24,000 a year.

If your gross margin is around 65% (I am guessing), you need £2,500 to £3,000 a month in **extra** sales just to break even. At £20 a candle, that is 125 to 150 candles a month on top of what you sell now.

**Risks, ranked by likelihood and impact**

1. **Seasonality (high likelihood, high impact).** Candle sales cluster in October to December. Rent is due every month. January to August could run at a loss of £1,000 or more each month.
2. **Lease lock-in (high likelihood, high impact).** Commercial leases often run 3 to 5 years and require a personal guarantee. A bad year then becomes your personal debt. Insist on a break clause at 12 months.
3. **Your time (high likelihood, medium impact).** A shop needs about 40 hours a week of opening. You either lose your weekend markets, which are your proven income, or you pay staff. At the National Living Wage of £12.21 an hour, 20 hours a week costs about £1,060 a month.
4. **Footfall that does not convert (medium likelihood, high impact).** Market shoppers come for the browsing. A shop needs its own passing trade, and high-street footfall is still weak in many towns.
5. **Fire and insurance terms (medium likelihood, medium impact).** Making candles on site may raise premiums or breach the lease's use clause. Check before signing.

**The most likely way it dies:** a slow February to July drains your cash. You stay open to avoid breaking the lease, then close at a loss with rent still owed.

**What I do not know:** your current monthly sales, your margin, the town, the lease terms, and whether you have online sales.

**What would have to be true for it to work:**
- Your markets already show at least £3,000 a month in surplus demand, such as regular sellouts or waiting lists.
- The lease is 12 months or has an early break clause, with no personal guarantee.
- Rates are zero after relief.
- You have 6 months of total costs, about £10,000 to £12,000, in reserve.

Cheaper tests come first: a 1 to 3 month pop-up, a shared unit, or a concession in an existing shop. Run one for a December and one for a quiet month.

**Bottom line:** Do not sign. Test demand with a short pop-up across a peak month and a quiet month, and only take a lease with a 12-month break and no personal guarantee.

### Advocate (Opportunity and upside)

**The usual answer:** Don't sign yet. Keep building market sales until you can prove the money.

I'll argue the bolder case, because the upside is real. A shop turns a weekend stall into a brand. You could sell six or seven days a week instead of two. Customers who like your candles can find you again. A fixed address also opens doors: local gift shops, corporate orders, hotel toiletries, and paid workshops. None of that works from a folding table.

Here are the numbers, with my assumptions stated:

- **Rent:** £1,200 a month, or £14,400 a year.
- **Other costs:** business rates, utilities, card fees, insurance. I don't have these. Get them from the landlord and your local council. You may qualify for small business rate relief, which can cut or remove rates if the rateable value is low. Check this.
- **Gross margin:** I'm assuming 60% on candles. Check your own figure.

At a 60% margin, rent alone needs about £2,000 of sales a month. With other costs, you may need £2,500 or more. I don't know your current market takings, so I can't tell you whether that's realistic.

**Where the upside is limited:** A unit on a quiet street may get little passing trade. Commercial leases in England often run three to five years. They can require a deposit of several months' rent and sometimes a personal guarantee. Those obligations last longer than a good season.

**The unusual move:** Don't sign a standard lease. Ask about a short let or a meanwhile lease, which is a short arrangement on an empty unit and is often cheaper. Or run a Christmas pop-up from mid-November to late December. It's already 2 November, so you have a real test window. If the pop-up beats your market weekends, you have evidence. If it doesn't, your loss is small.

**What I'm unsure of:** Your current monthly sales, your actual margin, footfall on the street you're considering, and whether the landlord will include a break clause. Get those before you sign anything.

Bottom line: The shop has real upside, but only if your sales already cover about £2,500 a month. Test it with a short pop-up before you take on a full lease.

Bottom line: Not yet. At £1,200 a month you need about £2,700 in sales just to break even, and the shop would not repay its cost within 12 months on realistic numbers, so test with a short pop-up first and decide by 1 March 2027.

## Chair's resolution

## Decision
Do not sign a £1,200 a month lease now. Spend November and December working Christmas markets and recording your takings. Then run a short pop-up or meanwhile lease in January or February, the quiet months, and decide on a permanent unit by 1 March 2027.

## Why
- **The real cost is higher than the rent.** It is likely £1,600 to £2,000 a month once you add service charge, insurance, utilities and card fees. If the landlord charges VAT on rent and you are not VAT-registered, you cannot reclaim it, so £1,200 becomes £1,440. That is likely, since the registration threshold is £90,000 turnover. Upfront you also face a deposit (usually refundable), legal fees and £1,000 to £5,000 of fit-out.
- **Break-even is high.** At a 60 to 65% margin you need about £2,600 to £3,000 of sales a month just to cover the unit. That is about 6 or 7 sales a day, and it pays you nothing.
- **You cannot staff it alone.** Six days of opening plus making candles plus markets is more hours than you have. Paying staff for 20 to 25 hours a week at about £12.71 an hour (the April 2026 rate, please confirm) costs £1,100 to £1,400 a month before employer costs. That wipes out the likely profit.
- **The timing is wrong.** A lease signed now would open in January, the weakest month, after missing Christmas.
- **There is no evidence yet.** Nobody, including you, has shown that your customers would follow you to a fixed address.

## Main risks
- **Personal guarantee and lease length.** Terms of 3 to 5 years can turn a bad year into personal debt.
- **Full repairing lease and dilapidations.** You could owe thousands of pounds to restore the unit when you leave.
- **Seasonality.** Candle sales peak from October to December, but rent is due every month.
- **Losing market income.** Every Saturday in the shop is a market day you give up.
- **Use clause and insurance.** Making candles on site may breach the lease or raise your premiums.
- **Business rates.** Full relief applies only below £12,000 rateable value in England, tapering to £15,000. Scotland and Wales differ. Check the unit before assuming zero.

## Honest criticism for the owner
You asked about the rent without giving your sales, margin or what you take home now. Those numbers decide this question, not the £1,200. If you do not know them, that is the first problem to fix. Also, a shop is not "markets indoors". It is a second full-time job.

## Next steps
1. **From now to 24 December:** work every Christmas market you can. Record daily takings, cost per candle and your hours. Work out your real gross margin and monthly take-home.
2. **By 20 November:** ask the landlord for the unit's rateable value (also on the gov.uk valuation search), VAT status, service charge, lease length, break clause, personal guarantee, repair terms, whether it is inside the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954, and whether candle-making is permitted. Have a solicitor read any draft.
3. **January to February 2027:** run a 4 to 8 week pop-up, meanwhile lease or shared unit for £500 to £1,500. Sign a permanent lease only if quiet-month sales reach about £3,000 a month without cutting market income, and the lease has a 12-month break and no personal guarantee.
